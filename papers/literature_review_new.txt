PAPER 1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:   (U)NFV — (Unified) Neural Finite Volume
Authors: Lichtle, Canesse, Fu, Zinat Matin, Delle Monache, Bayen (UC Berkeley)
Year:    2025
Venue:   arXiv:2505.23702 | NeurIPS Workshop 2025
Link:    https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23702

KEY IDEA
  Replaces the hand-designed numerical flux in a finite volume scheme with a
  learned one. Rather than approximating the solution field directly, the network
  outputs the inter-cell flux; the cell update is then carried out by exact flux
  differencing (U^{n+1}_i = U^n_i - dt/dx * (F_{i+1/2} - F_{i-1/2})). The
  "Unified" variant further unifies several classical FVM schemes under one
  parameterisation. Reported up to 10x lower error than first-order Godunov at
  the same grid resolution.

WHAT IS MODELED
  Forward PDE solution operator: initial condition u0(x) --> full trajectory
  u(x,t). Tested on: 1D Burgers equation, 1D LWR traffic flow model, and NGSIM
  real traffic data (making it one of the few works validated on real-world data).

ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
  - Input: stencil of cell-averages around interface i+1/2 (fixed stencil width,
    typically 3–5 cells).
  - Processor: small MLP (or shallow CNN) mapping stencil values to a scalar
    flux F_{i+1/2}.
  - Update: standard flux-differencing cell update (exact FVM step).
  - No attention, no global Fourier modes — purely local stencil operations.
  - Trained end-to-end on trajectory data with MSE loss.

IS IT A BLACK BOX?
  No.

PHYSICAL FEATURES EXPLICITLY MODELED
  - Discrete conservation (enforced structurally via flux differencing).
  - Locality (flux depends only on a fixed stencil — respects finite speed of
    propagation).
  No entropy stability, no WENO smoothness gating, no shock weighting.

HOW IT APPLIES TO US
  DIRECT COMPETITOR to HypFluxNO. Both learn a neural numerical flux inside a
  conservative FVM update. Key differences where HypFluxNO goes further:
    (a) HypFluxNO adds WENO smoothness indicators to gate the flux near shocks.
    (b) HypFluxNO includes an entropy dissipation loss.
    (c) HypFluxNO uses a space-time message-passing backbone rather than a
        fixed local stencil MLP.
  The (U)NFV paper is the strongest baseline to compare against.

FASTER THAN NUMERICAL METHODS?
  At test time: yes — a single forward pass through the flux MLP replaces
  iterative Riemann solver calls. The paper does not report explicit wall-clock
  speedups but notes ~10x error reduction at the same grid/time resolution,
  implying numerical accuracy is achieved with coarser (faster) grids.

PAPER 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:   GoRINNs — Godunov-Riemann Informed Neural Networks
Authors: Patsatzis, Russo, Siettos, Kevrekidis
Year:    2025
Venue:   Journal of Computational Physics, vol. 534
Link:    (search "GoRINNs JCP 2025" / check JCP vol 534)

KEY IDEA
  Embeds the Godunov scheme structure and Rankine-Hugoniot jump conditions
  directly into the architecture. Rather than learning an arbitrary function,
  each network "cell" computes an approximate Riemann solution at each interface
  using a learned but physically constrained flux. The Rankine-Hugoniot condition
  (shock speed = jump in flux / jump in state) is enforced as a hard architectural
  constraint rather than as a penalty term.

WHAT IS MODELED
  Forward trajectory operator for 1D scalar and 2x2 systems. Tested on:
  - Burgers equation (scalar conservation law)
  - Shallow Water equations (2x2 system)
  - LWR traffic flow model (scalar, same as our setup)
  - Payne-Whitham second-order traffic model (2x2 system)

ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
  - Shallow MLP (1–2 hidden layers) per interface, mapping left/right states to
    interface flux.
  - Architectural hard constraint: outputs satisfy Rankine-Hugoniot across shocks.
  - Cell update: exact Godunov-style flux differencing.
  - Minimal parameters — emphasis on interpretability over expressiveness.
  - Training: supervised on FVM ground truth.

IS IT A BLACK BOX?
  No.

PHYSICAL FEATURES EXPLICITLY MODELED
  - Conservation (flux differencing).
  - Rankine-Hugoniot jump condition (hard architectural constraint).
  - Entropy condition: partially — Godunov upwinding is entropy-stable for
    scalar convex fluxes, so the scheme inherits this for the scalar case.

HOW IT APPLIES TO US
  Most closely related to HypFluxNO in spirit: both learn a physical numerical
  flux and both test on LWR. GoRINNs' strength is its hard Rankine-Hugoniot
  enforcement and theoretical grounding. Its weakness is shallow architectures
  that may not scale to complex multi-modal ICs. HypFluxNO's WENO gating and
  space-time message passing are architectural extensions beyond GoRINNs.

FASTER THAN NUMERICAL METHODS?
  Yes for the forward pass: the learned flux MLP is evaluated in parallel over
  all interfaces vs. sequential Riemann solvers. Specific speedup numbers are
  not the paper's focus (accuracy is the primary claim).

PAPER 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:   clawNO — Conservation Law Neural Operator
Authors: Liu, Fan, Zeng, Klower, Zhang, Yu
Year:    2024 (Spotlight)
Venue:   ICML 2024
Link:    https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11176

KEY IDEA
  A backbone-agnostic wrapper that enforces exact discrete conservation in ANY
  neural operator. The network does not output the solution field u directly;
  instead it outputs an antisymmetric matrix A(x,x') such that the flux
  F_i = sum_j A_{ij} can be antisymmetrically cancelled in the cell update,
  guaranteeing div(F) = 0 by construction. This is the operator-learning analogue
  of the finite-volume divergence theorem.

WHAT IS MODELED
  General conservation laws — the framework is backbone-agnostic. Demonstrated
  on:
  - Incompressible Navier-Stokes (velocity field conservation).
  - 2D shallow water / radial dam break (mass and momentum conservation).
  - Atmospheric dynamics.
  - Material deformation (stress conservation).

ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
  - Core idea: replace direct u-output with antisymmetric flux kernel A(x,x').
  - The cell update is: du_i/dt = sum_j [A(x_i, x_j) - A(x_j, x_i)] * phi_j,
    which is zero-sum by antisymmetry — exact conservation.
  - Can wrap FNO, DeepONet, GNO, or any backbone as the kernel A.
  - Decoder outputs antisymmetric matrix entries rather than state values.

IS IT A BLACK BOX?
  No (conservation is structurally enforced).

PHYSICAL FEATURES EXPLICITLY MODELED
  - Exact discrete conservation (antisymmetric flux construction).
  No entropy stability, no shock-specific treatment, no upwinding.

HOW IT APPLIES TO US
  Architecturally parallel to HypFluxNO's antisymmetric flux MLP design. Both
  enforce conservation by construction via antisymmetric/skew-symmetric flux
  representations. The key distinction: clawNO is backbone-agnostic but does
  not add entropy or WENO structure; HypFluxNO is specifically tuned for
  hyperbolic shocks with WENO gating. clawNO did NOT test on shock-dominated
  problems with discontinuities — its SW test uses a dam break but no strong
  shock regime. This is a natural comparison point in any paper: "unlike clawNO,
  HypFluxNO additionally enforces entropy dissipation and smoothness-aware flux
  gating."

FASTER THAN NUMERICAL METHODS?
  Not reported as a primary claim. clawNO inherits whatever speedup the
  underlying NO backbone provides. For FNO backbone: ~10–100x faster than FEM
  at similar accuracy on smooth problems; shock regime speedup unclear.

PAPER 4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:   RiemannONets — Interpretable Neural Operators for Riemann Problems
Authors: Peyvan, Oommen, Jagtap, Karniadakis (Brown University)
Year:    2024
Venue:   Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, vol. 424
Link:    https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08886

KEY IDEA
  A modified DeepONet specifically designed for 1D Euler Riemann problems with
  extreme parameter regimes (pressure ratios up to 10^10). The trunk network's
  basis functions are orthonormalized via SVD-like decomposition so that each
  basis vector aligns with a physically meaningful wave family (shock, rarefaction,
  contact). Two-stage training: (1) unsupervised trunk pretraining for
  interpretable basis, (2) supervised branch training on Riemann data.

WHAT IS MODELED
  Solution operator for 1D Euler Riemann problems:
  - Input: left and right initial states (rho_L, u_L, p_L, rho_R, u_R, p_R).
  - Output: solution profile u(x, t=T) at a fixed final time.
  - Tests: extreme shock tubes, blast waves, pressure ratios 10^0 to 10^10.

ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
  - Branch network: encodes the left/right state pair into a latent vector.
  - Trunk network: maps spatial coordinate x to a set of orthonormal basis
    functions (SVD-orthonormalized at each training step).
  - Dot product of branch coefficients and trunk basis gives solution.
  - Optional: physics-informed second stage penalizing Euler residuals.
  - No graph structure, no Fourier modes.

IS IT A BLACK BOX?
  Partially. The trunk basis is made interpretable (wave families emerge), but
  conservation is NOT explicitly enforced. The network can violate Rankine-
  Hugoniot in edge cases.

PHYSICAL FEATURES EXPLICITLY MODELED
  - Interpretable wave-family basis (emergent, not hard constraint).
  - Optional PINN residual penalty for Euler equations.
  No hard conservation, no entropy stability.

HOW IT APPLIES TO US
  Less directly relevant to HypFluxNO's architecture but useful for comparison.
  RiemannONets shows that DeepONet-style approaches can handle extreme shocks
  if the trunk basis is structured around wave families. For our 1D scalar
  case (Burgers/LWR), the Riemann problem structure is simpler, but the idea
  of interpretable basis decomposition is related to understanding what our
  model learns. The 2-3 OOM speedup claim is a useful benchmark reference.

FASTER THAN NUMERICAL METHODS?
  Yes — 2 to 3 orders of magnitude faster than classical exact Riemann solvers
  at inference time. This is one of the few papers in this area with explicit
  wall-clock speedup numbers for hyperbolic problems.

PAPER 5
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:   NESCFN — Neural Entropy-Stable Conservative Flux Network
         (follow-up: SymCLaw, arXiv:2601.21080, Jan 2026)
Authors: Liu, Zhang, Gelb (Dartmouth / Rice University)
Year:    2025
Venue:   arXiv:2507.01795
Link:    https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01795

KEY IDEA
  The first neural operator framework to enforce BOTH conservation AND entropy
  stability by construction. Conservation is handled via flux differencing;
  entropy stability is achieved by parameterizing the numerical flux using
  Tadmor's entropy-stable flux splitting: F = (F^+ + F^-)/2 where F^+ and F^-
  are learned to satisfy the entropy inequality. The entropy function itself is
  represented by an Input-Convex Neural Network (ICNN), ensuring convexity
  (required for a valid entropy). The follow-up SymCLaw additionally guarantees
  hyperbolicity of the learned system.

WHAT IS MODELED
  Inverse problem (learns the governing flux from trajectory data) + forward
  evaluation. Tested on:
  - 1D Burgers equation.
  - 1D Shallow Water equations.
  - 1D Euler equations (Sod shock tube).
  Note: primarily an inverse/discovery framework, but the learned model can
  then be used as a forward operator.

ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
  - Entropy network: ICNN (Input-Convex NN) representing the convex entropy
    function eta(u). Convexity is enforced by positive weights + convex
    activations.
  - Flux network: MLP split into entropy-stable F^+ / F^- branches via Tadmor
    decomposition.
  - Cell update: exact flux differencing for conservation.
  - Training: supervised on trajectory data with entropy dissipation as a hard
    structural constraint (not a loss penalty).
  - SymCLaw extension: adds a hyperbolicity loss to ensure real eigenvalues.

IS IT A BLACK BOX?
  No.

PHYSICAL FEATURES EXPLICITLY MODELED
  - Exact discrete conservation (flux differencing).
  - Entropy stability (Tadmor-style flux splitting + ICNN convex entropy).
  - Hyperbolicity (SymCLaw extension only).

HOW IT APPLIES TO US
  Most directly relevant to HypFluxNO's entropy_loss component. NESCFN shows
  that entropy stability can be enforced AS A HARD ARCHITECTURAL CONSTRAINT
  (not just a soft penalty), which is strictly stronger than HypFluxNO's
  lambda_entropy loss. A natural future direction: replace HypFluxNO's soft
  entropy penalty with a Tadmor-structured flux + ICNN entropy. Key limitation
  of NESCFN for our use case: it is an inverse problem solver, not a forward
  operator that generalises across initial conditions. HypFluxNO is a true
  forward operator (IC --> trajectory).

FASTER THAN NUMERICAL METHODS?
  Not reported. The paper focuses on physical correctness and identifiability
  of the governing flux, not inference speed.

COMPARATIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

All five papers embed physical structure beyond black-box regression:

  Paper 1 (UNFV)      — conservation via flux diff. Direct competitor.
  Paper 2 (GoRINNs)   — conservation + Rankine-Hugoniot (hard). Best theory.
  Paper 3 (clawNO)    — conservation via antisymmetric kernel. Most general.
  Paper 4 (RiemannONets) — interpretable wave-family trunk. Fastest at inference.
  Paper 5 (NESCFN)    — conservation + entropy stability (hard). Best physics.

HypFluxNO sits at the intersection: it has conservation (flux diff, like all 5),
WENO smoothness gating (unique among these), entropy dissipation (soft, like
NESCFN but not as hard), and space-time message passing (richer than UNFV's
fixed stencil). The main gap vs. NESCFN is soft vs. hard entropy enforcement.
The main advantage vs. all 5 is the combination of WENO gating + entropy +
expressive backbone tested on a real traffic dataset (NGSIM, shared with UNFV).
